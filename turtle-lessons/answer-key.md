# TurtleLab Lessons answer key

**Step 1.** The L starts at (-150, 60) facing down (heading 180). It ends at (-90, -60), facing right (heading 90). A square frame takes 8 blocks: four `forward` and four `right`. In the L and I frame, the four blocks that walk the turtle from the L to the I are `pen up`, `set xy`, `set heading` and `pen down`. The turtle ends the L facing right, but the I still draws downward, because `set heading 180` turns the turtle to face down before the pen goes down. With the 180 above the I changed to 90, the I lies on its side and runs to the right (from (-40, 60) to (80, 60)), because the turtle faces right when it draws and a letter is drawn in whatever direction the turtle faces.

**Step 2.** Turns that close each shape (repeat, turn): triangle 3, 120. Pentagon 5, 72. Hexagon 6, 60. Octagon 8, 45. 12 sides: 12, 30. The turns of a closed shape add up to 360 because the turtle has to make one full turn to face the way it started. A square takes 8 blocks the long way and 3 with `repeat` (the `repeat`, one `forward`, one `right`). In the triangle frame a turn of 100 leaves it open (3 × 100 is 300). In the pentagon frame a `repeat` of 6 with a turn of 72 draws the sixth side over the first. In the sides frame, 3, 5 and 8 give a triangle, a pentagon and an octagon; 40 (a turn of 9) and 100 (a turn of 3.6) look like a circle; 2 draws a line out and back (a turn of 180).\n\n**Step 3.** The second picture does not match the first (the colors are random). Smallest 0, largest 255. 50 × 7.2 = 360, a full circle. With 100 rays the turn is 3.6. Two runs of the confetti do not match, because the places and colors are random.

A confetti that works:

```
start
clean
set pen size 30
repeat 60
    pen up
    set xy (random -350 350) (random -280 280)
    pen down
    set color (random 0 255) (random 0 255) (random 0 255)
    forward 1
```

In the confetti start frame the dots land in a thin band because the y places are picked from -20 to 20, and they are all red because `set color` is 255, 0, 0. The fixes are `random -280 280` for y (x is already `random -350 350`) and a `random 0 255` in each slot of `set color`.

**Step 4.** When the number is 1 the turtle turns right (90). In the `else` it turns left (90). It cannot do both and cannot do neither. Three runs differ, because `random` flips differently each time. Trace headings after each flip: 90, 180, 90, 180, 90, 0. The unfair coin: `random 1 4 = 1` (or `random 1 4 < 2`) in the pointed slot, with right in the top mouth.

**Steps 2 to 4 worksheet.** Step 2 table, the turns: hexagon 60, octagon 45, 12 sides 30; the rule is turn = 360 ÷ the number of sides. Step 3: smallest 0, largest 255; 50 × 7.2 = 360; 100 rays turn 3.6. Step 4, the six flips (1, 1, 2, 1, 2, 2), turn then heading: right 90 (90), right 90 (180), left 90 (90), right 90 (180), left 90 (90), left 90 (0). The last line is the student's own sentence, so nothing is checked by number.

**Step 5.** Nothing to check by number. The student page lists five things: a repeat that draws part of the picture, random that can be seen at work (two runs differ), an if and else that changes something visible, at least two colors, and a sticky note with their name and what the loop, the random and the if and else each do.

**Step 6.** The slow program (`lesson-box-steps`): after the first `store in box1` it holds 40; after the two `store in box1` blocks with a `+ 10` in the slot it holds 50, then 60; the two `print box1` blocks both print 60 and change nothing; `store in box1` with 100 replaces the 60 (it is gone); the last `print` prints 100; `forward box1` draws a line 100 long. The Output box shows 60, 60, 100. The three rules: a variable holds one value, storing replaces it, looking does not change it. The house program (`lesson-box-house`) stores 80 and uses `box1` in three `forward` blocks (the walls, the climb to the roof, the roof). Changing the 80 to 120 makes every side 120, and to 50 makes every side 50, changing one number. In the polygon sample `box1` is used twice: as the `repeat` count (the sides) and in the turn `360 ÷ box1`. Two variables for the Done early: `store in box1` the size, `store in box2` the sides, `repeat box2` with `forward box1` and `right (360 ÷ box2)`.

**Step 7.** Counting box (`lesson-box-trace`) prints 10, 20, 30, 40, 50, 60, 70, 80, so the first lap prints 10 and the last 80. With `box1 + 5` the last lap prints 40. With a `repeat` of 12 and `box1 + 10` the last prints 120. Doubling: starts at 2, three laps print 4, 8, 16, and five laps end at 64 (4, 8, 16, 32, 64). Countdown prints 3, 2, 1 and leaves 0 in the box. Starting at 5 with 3 laps prints 5, 4, 3 and leaves 2; with 5 laps it prints 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. The `store in box1` that makes the box bigger must be inside the `repeat`. A spiral with a turn of 120 makes a triangle spiral and 60 a hexagon spiral. The Done early swirl uses `box2` starting at 10, plus 2 each lap, in `right`.

**Step 8.** In `own-block` the sizes are 40, 80 and 120 and the turtle draws 12 lines (3 squares of 4). In the flower (`lesson-own-flower`) `petal` is used 8 times (the `repeat`), each petal is two arcs (16 arcs in all), and the turn is 45 because 8 × 45 = 360. With 6 petals the turn is 60 (6 × 60 = 360). Changing the 120 inside `to petal` to 90 changes all eight petals. A word of the student's own: any block under `to` with a `size` input, used three times with three sizes and a turn or move between them. In the B frame, the straight B has 15 blocks and the curved B has 9. The four blocks before each B are `pen up`, `set xy`, `set heading` and `pen down`. With the 0 changed to 90 above the curved B, the whole B turns a quarter turn to the right and its spine runs sideways, because the turtle ends the straight B facing left (heading 270) and a letter block draws in whatever direction the turtle faces. In the E and B frame, the walk from the E to the B is `pen up`, `forward 20`, `left 90` and `pen down`: the E ends facing right, so `forward 20` walks the turtle to the right of the E and `left 90` turns it to face up, where the B starts. The name: nothing is checked by number. Look for one block of their own for each different letter (a letter that comes twice uses the same block), no `set xy` inside a letter block, the four travel blocks before each letter in the main program, and a different color on every letter. A `set color` before a letter block colors that one use; inside the block it colors every use of that letter.

**Step 9.** Twelve squares (`lesson-nested-count`): 12 × 4 = 48 `forward` blocks, and the turn is 30 because 12 × 30 = 360. With 6 squares the turn is 60; with 36 squares it is 10. Twelve squares with a turn of 20 do not close: 12 × 20 = 240, two thirds of a circle. Hexagon flower: 6 × 6 = 36 lines. Pentagons: inside `repeat` 5 with a turn of 72, outside `repeat` 5 with a turn of 72 (25 lines). Both repeats at 20 is 20 × 20 = 400 lines. A `set color` inside the outside loop but outside the inside loop changes once per shape, not once per side. Nested squares prints 25, 50, 75, 100, 125, 150, 175, 200: the box grows by 25 each lap, so each square is 25 bigger than the one before.

**Step 10.** `0 255 0` green, `0 0 255` blue, `255 255 0` yellow, `255 255 255` white, `128 128 128` gray, `0 0 0` black on a black screen so the bar vanishes. Orange is about `255 128 0`. The switches are worth 128, 64, 32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1, which add up to 255. 255 is `1111 1111` (FF), 128 is `1000 0000` (80), 64 is `0100 0000` (40) and 10 is `0000 1010` (0A). `#FFFF00` is yellow and `#00FF00` is green. 8 bits make 2⁸ = 256 numbers. Three lights make 256 × 256 × 256 = 16,777,216 colors (24 bits). In the spinning squares the first square is `7 60 248` (strongest blue) and the last `252 60 3` (strongest red), because red is `box1` and blue is `255 - box1`, and the two always add up to 255.

**Step 11.** Bug 1: the turn is 100 and must be 120 (360 ÷ 3), because 3 × 100 = 300 is not a full turn. Bug 2: box1 holds 100 but the `forward` has the number 50 typed in, so nothing uses the box; plug a `box1` block into the `forward`. Bug 3: `random 1 2 = 3` is never true, so the program always takes the `else` and always turns left, tracing the same small square; change the 3 to a 1 or a 2. Bug 4: `pen up` was used to climb to the roof and `pen down` was never used again, so the roof is walked but not drawn; add `pen down` after the climbing `forward`, before the roof. The four steps: run it, say what you expected, find the difference, change one thing.

**Step 12.** Nothing to check by number. The student page lists five things: a block of their own with an input that is used more than once, a box that changes while the program runs, a loop, at least two colors, and a sticky note with their name. The last page of the PDF shows a Count column that should have at least 1 for Variables, repeat / forever and Blocks you made.

**Step 13.** Typing 5 draws five sides with a turn of 360 ÷ 5 = 72, and box1 holds 5 afterwards. Typing 0 draws nothing, because the `repeat` runs 0 times. The answer always goes in box1, and a second `ask` replaces the first answer. The trace (type 50, then 5): when the `repeat` starts box1 is 5 and box2 is 50, and it draws a pentagon with sides of 50. Without the `store in box2` block, box2 stays 0, so `forward box2` goes forward 0 and nothing is drawn. The PDF button runs a program that has not been run, and an ask answers 0 there, so the drawing is empty: the student should run it and answer first.

**Step 14.** Clicking the middle gives about 0 and 0. The paper runs from -350 to 350 across and -280 to 280 up and down, so the top right corner is about 350 and 280 (both positive). The stamped square has its bottom left corner at the click: the turtle goes to the click facing up, goes forward 30, and then turns right, so the square goes up and to the right of the click. The program keeps listening after its last block until Stop is pressed, and a slide of the paper is not a click.

**Step 15.** Nothing to check by number. The student page lists six things: an ask block whose answer is used, a when paper clicked that draws where the click was using mouseX and mouseY, a block of their own with an input that is used more than once, a loop, at least two colors, and a sticky note with their name. The last page of the PDF should show a Count of at least 2 for the row ask / when paper clicked, and at least 1 for repeat / forever and Blocks you made.

**Step 16.** The stripe covers x from 100 to 220. At x = 160 both questions are yes, so `and` says yes and it prints 1. At x = 300 the question `mouseX < 220` is no, so it prints 0. At x = 50 the question `mouseX > 100` is no, so it prints 0 (one no is enough for `and`). The `or` frame prints the opposite on every click, because outside means less than 100 or more than 220: at x = 160 both are no, so it prints 0; at x = 300 the second is yes, so it prints 1. A `not` around the `and` of the first frame says the opposite of it (at x = 160, `and` says yes and `not` says no). The corner at (50, -30): a click at (80, 10) is 30 right and 40 above, so it is in the square; a click at (80, 50) is 80 above, which is not less than 60, so the question about mouseY says no; a click at (40, 10) is 10 left of the corner, so `mouseX - box2` is -10, which is not more than 0. In the game, a hit adds 1 to box1 and runs `target`, which wipes the paper (`clean`), picks a new place in box2 and box3 and draws the square; a miss changes nothing, and a click that is in the right columns but the wrong row (or the reverse) is a miss. The student page lists six things: a square that jumps after every hit and not after a miss, a score in box1 that goes up by 1 for each hit, an `if` that uses `and`, a block of their own used twice, a `repeat`, and a sticky note with their name. The last page of the PDF should show a Count of at least 1 for repeat / forever and for Blocks you made.

**Step 17.** Three up arrows draw a line 60 long (each press walks 20). A left arrow does not move the turtle, it turns it a quarter turn to the left on the spot. Then two up arrows walk 40 to the left, so the turtle ends at x = -40 and y = 60. The spiral's timer is 3, in tenths, so 0.3 of a second between ticks. Its ticks walk 8, 16 and 24, and box1 is 16, 24 and 32 after them, so the third tick walks 24 and leaves 32 in box1. For the snake with the square's corner at (50, -30): at x = 70, y = 10 it is 20 right of and 40 above the corner, so both questions are yes and it is on the square; at x = 70, y = 50 it is 80 above the corner, so the question about y says no (80 is not less than 60); at x = 10, y = 10 it is 40 left of the corner, so x minus box1 is -40 and the question about x says no (-40 is not more than -30); at x = 340, y = 0 the question `x > 330` is yes, so the `or` says yes and the game starts again. The student page lists six things to check: the snake moves by itself and the arrow keys turn it, eating makes it fatter and a new square appears (the eaten one stays on the paper: only the newest square is remembered), off the paper starts the game again, a `when key pressed`, an `every`, an `and` and an `or`, two blocks of their own that are used, and a sticky note with their name. The last page of the PDF should show a Count of at least 5 for the row `ask / when paper clicked / when key pressed / every` (four key blocks and an every), and at least 2 for Blocks you made.

**Step 18.** Nothing to check by number: every game is different. The student page lists seven things: at least two of a click handler, a key handler and an every block, a box that changes while the program runs, an `if` that uses `and` or `or`, a block of their own used more than once, a loop, at least two colors, and a sticky note with their name saying what the game is and how to play it. The last page of the PDF should show a Count of at least 2 for the row `ask / when paper clicked / when key pressed / every`, at least 1 for `repeat / forever` and `if / if else`, and at least 1 for Blocks you made. A key does nothing until the page has the keyboard (press Run, click the paper once), and an `every` with a number smaller than 0.5 or an empty box stops with a message.
