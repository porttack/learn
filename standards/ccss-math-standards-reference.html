What this is. A locally built index of Common Core State Standards for Mathematics (National Governors Association Center for Best Practices, Council of Chief State School Officers) (2010). Scope. Middle school (6-8) and a grounded slice of high school Algebra/Functions -- only the strands and specific codes a real read of Bootstrap:Algebra's own 28-lesson sequence (bootstrapworld.org, Fall 2026 course page) actually supports, not every code its domain could theoretically cover. Skipped entirely, for lack of any matching lesson: A-SSE (quadratics/exponential series), A-REI.1/2/4 (quadratics), F-IF.3-6/8-9 (sequences, complex curve analysis, quadratic/exponential forms), F-BF.2-4 (sequences, transformations, inverses), F-LE.3-4 (exponential-specific), and all of grades 6-8 Statistics & Probability (no lesson in the sequence collects or analyzes real-world data). #Grade 6: The Number System

  • #6.NS.5 Use positive and negative numbers to represent quantities with opposite directions or values (temperature, elevation, debits/credits), explaining what 0 means in context.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 5 (Simple Data Types) (partial)
      "Simple Data Types" covers real-world inequalities with Pyret's Number type, which plausibly includes signed values (negative scores, position deltas), but the lesson description doesn't name this specifically.
  • #6.NS.6 Understand rational numbers as points on a number line, extend number lines and coordinate axes to negative numbers, and locate points in all four quadrants of the coordinate plane.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 2 (Coordinates), Chapter 4 (Game Design)
      "Coordinates" has students play a video game to identify how coordinates describe position; "Game Design" has them use coordinates for their own game's positions -- both are this standard directly.
  • #6.NS.7 Understand ordering and absolute value of rational numbers, including that absolute value is a number's distance from 0.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 5 (Simple Data Types), Chapter 22 (Simple Inequalities)
      "Simple Data Types" introduces real-world inequalities with ordered/signed values; "Simple Inequalities" has students identify solutions and non-solutions, which depends on ordering and absolute value.
  • #6.NS.8 Solve real-world problems by graphing points in all four quadrants of the coordinate plane, using coordinates and absolute value to find distances between points sharing a coordinate.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 27 (Distance in Video Games)
      "Distance in Video Games" has students find distances between coordinate points, the applied form of this standard.

#Grade 7: Expressions & Equations

  • #7.EE.1 Use properties of operations to add, subtract, factor, and expand linear expressions with rational coefficients.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 6 (Order of Operations)
      "Order of Operations," using Bootstrap's own "Circles of Evaluation" model, is generating and reading equivalent arithmetic expressions.
  • #7.EE.2 Recognize that rewriting an expression in a different form can clarify a problem and how its quantities relate.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 8 (Function Composition) (partial)
      Rewriting an expression to see how quantities relate is the same move "Function Composition" makes with image-transformation functions, though the lesson's own framing is programming, not algebraic rewriting.
  • #7.EE.3 Solve multi-step real-life problems with rational numbers in any form, using properties of operations and checking the reasonableness of answers.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 6 (Order of Operations), Chapter 19 (Solving Word Problems with the Design Recipe)
      "Order of Operations" and "Solving Word Problems with the Design Recipe" (multi-step problems, checked via the Design Recipe's own verification step) both match this standard directly.
  • #7.EE.4 Use variables to represent quantities, write equations and inequalities to solve real-world problems, and graph and interpret inequality solution sets.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 19 (Solving Word Problems with the Design Recipe), Chapter 21 (Problem Decomposition), Chapter 22 (Simple Inequalities), Chapter 24 (Sam the Butterfly — Applying Inequalities)
      Word problems solved via the Design Recipe (19), revenue/cost/profit problems (21, "Problem Decomposition"), and inequalities applied to keep a game character on screen (22, 24) are this standard's equation- and inequality-writing both.

#Grade 8: Functions

  • #8.F.1 Understand a function as a rule that assigns exactly one output to each input; a function's graph is the set of ordered pairs of input and output.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 7 (Contracts for Strings and Images), Chapter 13 (Functions Make Life Easier!), Chapter 14 (The Vertical Line Test), Chapter 16 (Functions: Contracts, Examples & Definitions), Chapter 20 (Functions for Character Animation)
      "Contracts for Strings and Images," "Functions Make Life Easier!," "The Vertical Line Test," "Functions: Contracts, Examples & Definitions," and "Functions for Character Animation" are this course's core, repeated treatment of a function as a rule from input to output.
  • #8.F.2 Compare properties of two functions represented in different ways (algebraically, graphically, in a table, or by verbal description).
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 17 (Functions Can Be Linear), Chapter 18 (Defining Linear Functions)
      "Functions Can Be Linear" and "Defining Linear Functions" both explore slope and intercept using definitions, tables, and graphs -- comparing representations of the same function.
  • #8.F.3 Interpret the equation y = mx + b as defining a linear function, and give examples of functions that are not linear.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 17 (Functions Can Be Linear), Chapter 18 (Defining Linear Functions)
      Same two lessons: reading y = mx + b as a linear function via slope and intercept is this standard directly.

#High School: Interpreting Functions

  • #F-IF.7 Graph functions expressed symbolically, including piecewise-defined functions (step and absolute value functions among them), showing key features.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 25 (Piecewise Functions and Conditionals), Chapter 26 (Player Animation)

#High School: Reasoning with Equations & Inequalities

  • #A-REI.3 Solve linear equations and inequalities in one variable, including those with coefficients given as letters.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 19 (Solving Word Problems with the Design Recipe), Chapter 22 (Simple Inequalities)
      Solving one-variable linear equations (19) and identifying solutions/non-solutions of one-variable inequalities (22) are this standard directly.
  • #A-REI.10 Understand that the graph of an equation in two variables is the set of all its solutions plotted in the coordinate plane.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 17 (Functions Can Be Linear), Chapter 18 (Defining Linear Functions)
      Graphing linear functions as the set of (x, y) pairs satisfying y = mx + b is this standard's own definition of a two-variable equation's graph.
  • #A-REI.12 Graph the solution to a linear inequality in two variables as a half-plane, and graph the solution to a system of linear inequalities as the intersection of the corresponding half-planes.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 22 (Simple Inequalities), Chapter 23 (Compound Inequalities: Solutions & Non-Solutions), Chapter 24 (Sam the Butterfly — Applying Inequalities)
      "Simple Inequalities," "Compound Inequalities" (union and intersection), and "Sam the Butterfly" (keeping a character on screen) are this standard's half-plane and system-of-inequalities graphing.

#High School: Linear & Exponential Models

  • #F-LE.1 Distinguish situations modeled by linear functions (equal differences over equal intervals) from those modeled by exponential functions (equal factors over equal intervals).
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 17 (Functions Can Be Linear), Chapter 18 (Defining Linear Functions) (partial)
      The linear-function lessons teach recognizing a constant-rate (linear) relationship, this standard's part (b); nothing in the course distinguishes linear from exponential growth, part (a)/(c), so this is a partial match to one part of a three-part standard.
  • #F-LE.2 Construct linear and exponential functions from a graph, a description of a relationship, or two input-output pairs.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 17 (Functions Can Be Linear), Chapter 18 (Defining Linear Functions)
      Building a linear function from a table, graph, or two points is exactly what these two lessons teach.
  • #F-LE.5 Interpret the parameters of a linear or exponential function (e.g. slope, intercept) in terms of the context it models.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 17 (Functions Can Be Linear), Chapter 18 (Defining Linear Functions), Chapter 19 (Solving Word Problems with the Design Recipe), Chapter 21 (Problem Decomposition)
      Interpreting slope and intercept in context (a per-unit rate, a starting value) runs through the linear-function lessons, the Design Recipe word problems, and the revenue/cost/profit problem decomposition.
