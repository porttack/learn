#Grade 6: The Number System

  • #6.NS.5 Use positive and negative numbers to represent quantities with opposite directions or values (temperature, elevation, debits/credits), explaining what 0 means in context.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 2 (Coordinates), Chapter 5 (Simple Data Types), Chapter 24 (Sam the Butterfly — Applying Inequalities) (partial)
      The Math Inside Video Games page says negative coordinates can hide a character off screen, Simple Data Types has students type negative integers, fractions and decimals into the editor, and Sam the Butterfly asks how far left of 0 Sam may go. Signed numbers are used, but the lessons do not ask for meaning of 0 or opposite quantities in context.
  • #6.NS.6 Understand rational numbers as points on a number line, extend number lines and coordinate axes to negative numbers, and locate points in all four quadrants of the coordinate plane.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 2 (Coordinates), Chapter 4 (Game Design), Chapter 11 (Transforming and Composing Images), Chapter 27 (Distance in Video Games)
      Estimating Coordinates ('Coordinates'), the Brainstorm Your Own Game screen with its corners labeled (0,0) to (640,480) ('Game Design'), 'Making Sense of Coordinates' with translate on graph paper ('Transforming and Composing Images') and distances between points with negative coordinates such as (9, -3) ('Distance in Video Games'). Most of the work is in the first quadrant, so negative axes appear mainly in 'Distance in Video Games'.
  • #6.NS.7 Understand ordering and absolute value of rational numbers, including that absolute value is a number's distance from 0.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 5 (Simple Data Types), Chapter 22 (Simple Inequalities), Chapter 27 (Distance in Video Games)
      Simple Data Types has predicted comparisons of numbers with <, <=, >=, <> (Booleans page); Simple Inequalities finds solutions and non-solutions; Line Length ('Distance in Video Games') asks 'why is the distance between two points always positive?' and builds distance with conditionals, which is absolute value as distance from 0.
  • #6.NS.8 Solve real-world problems by graphing points in all four quadrants of the coordinate plane, using coordinates and absolute value to find distances between points sharing a coordinate.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 27 (Distance in Video Games)
      "Distance in Video Games" has students find distances between coordinate points, the applied form of this standard.

#Grade 7: Expressions & Equations

  • #7.EE.1 Use properties of operations to add, subtract, factor, and expand linear expressions with rational coefficients.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 6 (Order of Operations), Chapter 10 (Surface Area of a Rectangular Prism), Chapter 21 (Problem Decomposition)
      Circles of Evaluation ('Order of Operations') turn expressions into code and back; 'Surface Area: More than One Way' ('Surface Area of a Rectangular Prism') and 'Profit: More than one Way' ('Problem Decomposition') have students write several equivalent expressions for one quantity and check that they agree.
  • #7.EE.2 Recognize that rewriting an expression in a different form can clarify a problem and how its quantities relate.

      Rewriting an expression in another form to see how its quantities relate is the point of 'Surface Area: More than One Way' (2AB+2BC+2AC against 2(AB+BC+AC), double the sum of three faces) and the four equivalent profit definitions ('Problem Decomposition').   • #7.EE.3 Solve multi-step real-life problems with rational numbers in any form, using properties of operations and checking the reasonableness of answers.

  • #7.EE.4 Use variables to represent quantities, write equations and inequalities to solve real-world problems, and graph and interpret inequality solution sets.
    • Bootstrap:Algebra: Chapter 19 (Solving Word Problems with the Design Recipe), Chapter 21 (Problem Decomposition), Chapter 22 (Simple Inequalities), Chapter 24 (Sam the Butterfly — Applying Inequalities)
