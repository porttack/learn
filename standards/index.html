AP Computer Science Principles

Big Idea 1: Creative Development (10–13%)
1.1 · Collaboration
Working with other people, and giving them credit, changes how a computing project turns out.
1.2 · Program Function and Purpose
A program exists to do something for someone, and describing what it does means naming its inputs, its outputs, and the behavior that connects them.
Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 1 (Welcome), Chapter 5 (Conditionals and Recursion)
1.3 · Program Design and Development
Building a program well means planning before writing, documenting while writing, and being willing to revise.
Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 4 (Functions and Interfaces)
1.4 · Identifying and Correcting Errors
Programs fail in a few recognizable ways, and testing with well-chosen inputs -- including edge cases -- is how those failures get found before a user does.
Covered by CS50 AP -- extended (weeks 0-6 remainder): Unit 2 (Week 2: Arrays); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 1 (Welcome), Chapter 2 (Variables and Statements), Chapter 3 (Functions), Chapter 4 (Functions and Interfaces), Chapter 5 (Conditionals and Recursion), Chapter 6 (Return Values), Chapter 7 (Iteration and Search), Chapter 8 (Strings and Regular Expressions), Chapter 9 (Lists), Chapter 10 (Dictionaries), Chapter 11 (Tuples), Chapter 12 (Text Analysis and Generation), Chapter 13 (Files and Databases)
Big Idea 2: Data (17–22%)
2.1 · Binary Numbers
Computers store everything as bits, and binary place-value works the same way decimal place-value does, just base 2 instead of base 10.
Covered by CS50 AP -- taught subset (weeks 0, 3, 4, 7-9): Unit 0 (Week 0: Scratch -- data representation only (unary, binary, decimal, ASCII, Unicode, RGB)), Unit 4 (Week 4: Memory -- data representation only (hexadecimal, images, file I/O)); Covered by Working in Python: Interlude B (Representing Data, outline only)
2.2 · Data Compression
Compression trades off file size against fidelity; lossless keeps everything, lossy throws some of it away for a bigger size reduction.
Covered by Working in Python: Interlude B (Representing Data, outline only)
2.3 · Extracting Information from Data
Raw data becomes information once a program finds the patterns in it, and real data sets are rarely clean enough to do that without some processing first.
Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 12 (Text Analysis and Generation), Chapter 13 (Files and Databases)
2.4 · Using Programs with Data
Programs that read, combine, and rewrite data files let a person do with a data set what would be impractical by hand.
Covered by CS50 AP -- taught subset (weeks 0, 3, 4, 7-9): Unit 7 (Week 7: SQL); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 12 (Text Analysis and Generation), Chapter 13 (Files and Databases)
Big Idea 3: Algorithms and Programming (30–35%)
3.1 · Variables and Assignments
A variable is a named place to keep a value, and assignment is how that name gets pointed at a value.
Covered by CS50 AP -- extended (weeks 0-6 remainder): Unit 1 (Week 1: C), Unit 6 (Week 6: Python); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 2 (Variables and Statements)
3.2 · Data Abstraction
Grouping related values under one name lets a program treat a collection as a single thing instead of tracking each part separately.
Covered by CS50 AP -- extended (weeks 0-6 remainder): Unit 2 (Week 2: Arrays), Unit 5 (Week 5: Data Structures); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 9 (Lists), Chapter 10 (Dictionaries), Chapter 11 (Tuples)
3.3 · Mathematical Expressions
Arithmetic and comparison operators combine into expressions the same way in code as they do on paper, with the same rules about order of operations.
Covered by CS50 AP -- extended (weeks 0-6 remainder): Unit 1 (Week 1: C); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 1 (Welcome), Chapter 2 (Variables and Statements)
3.4 · Strings
Text is data too, and a string is handled as a sequence of characters that can be measured, sliced, and searched.
Covered by CS50 AP -- extended (weeks 0-6 remainder): Unit 2 (Week 2: Arrays); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 1 (Welcome), Chapter 8 (Strings and Regular Expressions)
3.5 · Boolean Expressions
An expression that evaluates to true or false is what a program's decisions are built on.
Covered by CS50 AP -- extended (weeks 0-6 remainder): Unit 6 (Week 6: Python); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 5 (Conditionals and Recursion)
3.6 · Conditionals
An if-statement lets a program take one path or another depending on whether a condition holds.
Covered by CS50 AP -- extended (weeks 0-6 remainder): Unit 1 (Week 1: C), Unit 6 (Week 6: Python); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 5 (Conditionals and Recursion)
3.7 · Nested Conditionals
Putting one conditional inside another lets a program handle more than two possible outcomes.
Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 5 (Conditionals and Recursion)
3.8 · Iteration
Repeating a block of statements, either a fixed number of times or until some condition changes, is how a program does the same kind of work over and over.
Covered by CS50 AP -- extended (weeks 0-6 remainder): Unit 1 (Week 1: C), Unit 6 (Week 6: Python); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 3 (Functions), Chapter 7 (Iteration and Search)
3.9 · Developing Algorithms
Building an algorithm from scratch usually means starting with a rough version and refining it in small, testable steps.
Covered by CS50 AP -- taught subset (weeks 0, 3, 4, 7-9): Unit 3 (Week 3: Algorithms (searching, sorting, asymptotic notation, recursion)); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 6 (Return Values), Chapter 7 (Iteration and Search)
3.10 · Lists
A list holds an ordered sequence of values that a program can step through one at a time.
Covered by CS50 AP -- extended (weeks 0-6 remainder): Unit 2 (Week 2: Arrays), Unit 5 (Week 5: Data Structures); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 9 (Lists)
3.11 · Binary Search
Searching a sorted collection by repeatedly cutting the remaining range in half finds a value in far fewer steps than checking every element.
Covered by CS50 AP -- taught subset (weeks 0, 3, 4, 7-9): Unit 3 (Week 3: Algorithms (searching, sorting, asymptotic notation, recursion))
3.12 · Calling Procedures
Calling a function hands control to a separate block of code and, often, gets a value back in return.
Covered by CS50 AP -- extended (weeks 0-6 remainder): Unit 2 (Week 2: Arrays), Unit 6 (Week 6: Python); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 3 (Functions), Chapter 6 (Return Values)
3.13 · Developing Procedures
Writing a function well means giving it parameters general enough to be reused, and a name and docstring that explain what it does without exposing how.
Covered by CS50 AP -- extended (weeks 0-6 remainder): Unit 2 (Week 2: Arrays), Unit 6 (Week 6: Python); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 3 (Functions), Chapter 4 (Functions and Interfaces)
3.14 · Libraries
Importing a library brings in code someone else already wrote and tested, so a program can use it without reimplementing it.
Covered by CS50 AP -- extended (weeks 0-6 remainder): Unit 1 (Week 1: C), Unit 6 (Week 6: Python); Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 2 (Variables and Statements), Chapter 4 (Functions and Interfaces), Chapter 8 (Strings and Regular Expressions)
3.15 · Random Values
A program can introduce controlled unpredictability by drawing values from a random-number generator instead of computing them.
Covered by Working in Python: Chapter 12 (Text Analysis and Generation)
3.16 · Simulations
A simulation stands in for a real process that would be too slow, risky, or expensive to run for real, trading some realism for repeatability.
3.17 · Algorithmic Efficiency
Algorithms that solve the same problem are not equally fast, and how an algorithm's running time grows with input size is what separates a practical solution from an impractical one.
Covered by CS50 AP -- taught subset (weeks 0, 3, 4, 7-9): Unit 3 (Week 3: Algorithms (searching, sorting, asymptotic notation, recursion))
3.18 · Undecidable Problems
Some yes-or-no problems have no algorithm that can always answer correctly, no matter how much time is allowed.

Covered by CS50 AP -- extended (weeks 0-6 remainder): Unit 5 (Week 5: Data Structures)

HS-ALG-ML-06

HS-ALG-IM-09

HS-ALG-IM-10

HS-ALG-IM-11
Every algorithmic system's design quietly favors some outcomes over others, and naming which values -- efficiency, privacy, fairness -- got prioritized is part of understanding it.
