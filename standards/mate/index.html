MATE ROV Competition: What It Builds

A cross-reference compiled here from MATE's competition manuals for Scout, Navigator and Ranger. It is not a standard and not tied to one year. About this catalog.
Start: skills and competenciesWhat MATE says it buildsCompetencies in fullStandards mapSkill by skillSkills and Ocean 10 chartThe Ocean DecadeThe classes

MATE says its competition builds eighteen skills, nine for the workplace and nine specific to ROVs. Under each skill below are the competencies this site finds behind it. Click a bubble for what it means, the standards it draws on, and where this site teaches it.

Bubble colors: blue for engineering practice (ENG), green for professional and team skills (PRO), teal for ocean and environmental understanding (UND). A darker bubble means a lesson here teaches it. One competency can sit under several skills.

Employability skills (Evaluate-Compete general outcomes)

Communication
Explains the engineering process and the design to a broad audience, uses images, diagrams and data well, and writes clearly. Skill by skill
PRO.3 · Communicate technical work in writing and in person
Tell the story of how the design was reasoned and tested in a spec sheet, technical documentation and an engineering presentation with questions, so a reader or judge new to it can follow.
Develops: Technical Presentation, Communication
Navigator adds: The presentation is longer (15 minutes in the 2025 manual).
Ranger adds: The presentation is longer (the Scout manual describes Ranger as 15 minutes).
9-12.AP.22
As a program's design takes shape, writing the decisions down -- in whatever form fits -- is what makes the process legible to anyone else who looks at it later.
Writing design decisions down makes the process readable to anyone later.
SL.8.4 · Presentation of Knowledge and Ideas
Present claims and findings clearly and logically, emphasizing salient points with relevant evidence and well-chosen details, with appropriate eye contact, volume, and pronunciation.
Presenting findings clearly with evidence is the engineering presentation.
SL.9-10.4 · Presentation of Knowledge and Ideas
Present information, findings, and supporting evidence clearly, concisely, and logically, with organization, development, and style appropriate to purpose, audience, and task.
The same, with tighter organization for the audience.
W.8.2 · Text Types and Purposes
Write informative/explanatory texts that examine a topic and convey ideas, concepts, and information through the selection, organization, and analysis of relevant content.
Writing an informative text that explains a design clearly is the technical documentation.
W.9-10.2 · Text Types and Purposes
Write informative/explanatory texts to examine and convey complex ideas, concepts, and information clearly and accurately through the effective selection, organization, and analysis of content.
The same, with more complex ideas and organization.
