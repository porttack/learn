AP Networking Reference

What this is. A personal working reference for a brand-new, still-piloting AP course (the other half of AP Career Kickstart, alongside AP Cybersecurity) — built to understand what it covers, not wired into this site's standards catalog/carrier system. Units and topic titles are the College Board's own; skill descriptions below are an original paraphrase, not quoted framework text. Source: the official AP Networking Course Framework (for use beginning with the 2026-27 school year pilot).
Status: this is a pilot, not a general-availability course. Fall 2026 begins the third and final pilot year. Only schools that already participated in a prior AP Networking or AP Cybersecurity pilot may join this round. The pilot exam runs May 2027; full national launch (and the credential) follows in 2027-28. No prerequisite — aligns with CTE Digital Technology Pathways, built with Cisco and other industry partners, and aligned to the NICE Workforce Framework.
College course equivalentOne-semester college intro-to-networking course
PrerequisitesNone. Open to any motivated student willing to work individually and in teams.
LengthYearlong, 4 units (the published framework leaves the class-period pacing for each topic as an unfilled placeholder — a sign the document is still mid-draft for this pilot round)
2026-27 pilot examDigital, multiple-choice only, administered May 2027. Question count and timing not yet published.

Course Skills

Four skill categories, spiraled across all four units. Each has four progressively deeper sub-skills (A–D) — the same A-through-D shape AP Cybersecurity uses.

Skill Category 1Skill Category 2Skill Category 3Skill Category 4
Connect and Configure
Get data moving through computer networks.		 Secure
Apply protective, detective, and deterrent security controls.		 Troubleshoot
Diagnose degraded performance or functionality, and test fixes.		 Collaborate
Work with others, and with AI, to get a task done.
1.A Name common device and network components, protocols, and configurations; explain how they relate. 2.A Name vulnerabilities and their impact on data, devices, and networks; explain how security controls (with or without AI) address and monitor them. 3.A Name common device and network problems, and explain how tools (including AI) help diagnose their cause. 4.A Set clear, shared team objectives for a networking task.
1.B Work out, with or without AI help, the right configuration and settings for a device to get connectivity, management, and performance. 2.B Work out, with or without AI help, strategies to address a device or network vulnerability. 3.B Work out, with or without AI help, the cause of a device or network problem and a possible fix. 4.B Assign clear roles and responsibilities across a team.
1.C Implement and document a device/network configuration to establish and keep connectivity. 2.C Implement and document security controls that address a vulnerability and monitor the network. 3.C Implement and document a fix for a problem. 4.C Use AI as a collaboration tool, both solo and as a group.
1.D Verify connectivity and configuration, and troubleshoot anything that's still wrong. 2.D Verify a control actually mitigates the vulnerability without breaking access or availability, and troubleshoot as needed. 3.D Verify a fix worked, and keep troubleshooting if it didn't. 4.D Finish your share of a collaborative networking task.

Exam Format

The College Board has published very little about exam structure so far — expected, since this course is still in its final pilot year before 2027-28 general availability.

What's confirmed (official)
2026-27 pilot exam
(May 2027)		Digital, multiple-choice only. Built around “career-connected scenarios” that ask students to make evidence-based, contextually aware decisions. Question count, timing, and skill weighting not disclosed.
2027-28 operational examNo official format published yet.
Unverified, from third-party AP test-prep sites (not an official College Board page): the 2027-28 exam is expected to add a free-response section analyzing real networking artifacts and evidence, alongside the multiple-choice section. Also reported: a qualifying score on the 2026-27 pilot exam carries a free CompTIA certification exam voucher, and the course is designed to stack toward CompTIA Network+, Cisco CCNA, and CCST Networking. Treat these as rumor-grade until AP Central confirms them directly.
