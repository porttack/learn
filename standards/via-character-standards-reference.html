VIA Character Strengths Standards Reference

What this is. A locally built index of The VIA Classification of Character Strengths and Virtues (VIA Institute on Character; originating research by Christopher Peterson and Martin Seligman, Character Strengths and Virtues: A Handbook and Classification, 2004) (2004). Scope. VIA's classification is a general character-strengths taxonomy, not a CS-specific or even SEL-specific one. Unlike CASEL/CDE's T-SEL/RULER, which group skills by emotional or social competency, VIA groups strengths by classical virtue, and everyone is understood to carry all 24 strengths to some degree rather than mastering them in sequence. Teamwork, Leadership, Perseverance, Judgment, and Creativity read as likely strong candidates for a future carrier alignment pass against this project's existing Kagan/ROV Robotics content. Codes. Codes are this project's own sequential numbering per virtue (<virtue-abbreviation>.<n>), not an official VIA scheme -- VIA names its 24 strengths but doesn't number them. What this is not. #Wisdom

Strengths that involve acquiring and using knowledge well.

  • #WIS.1 Creativity Coming up with ideas that are both original and genuinely useful, not just different for its own sake.

    Unassigned

  • #WIS.2 Curiosity Actively seeking out new experiences and information rather than waiting for them to show up.

    Unassigned

  • #WIS.3 Judgment Weighing a decision from multiple angles, including evidence that cuts against what you already believe.
    • porttack.com: Society (Impact of Computing): Prove Me Wrong: A Socratic Seminar on AI and Cheating (partial)
      Judgment -- weighing a decision from multiple angles, including evidence that cuts against what you already believe -- is what the before/after temperature check is built to surface, though the seminar format exercises it more than it explicitly teaches it as a named skill.
  • #WIS.4 Love of Learning Being motivated to build real depth in a subject or skill, not just to get through it.

    Unassigned

  • #WIS.5 Perspective Being the person others come to for advice because you can see the bigger picture, drawing on your own experience to help someone else see it too.

    Unassigned

#Courage

Strengths that involve exercising will to accomplish goals in the face of difficulty.

  • #COU.1 Bravery Acting on what you believe is right even when it's risky, difficult, or frightening.
    • Little Brother (a book by Cory Doctorow): Chapter 17 (Barbara Stratford and the Crypto Wars)
      "I am so sick of being scared... Let them take me away. At least I'll have a little certainty in my life" is Marcus choosing to act on what he believes is right specifically because it's risky and frightening, not despite having worked out a safe way around that.
  • #COU.2 Honesty Presenting yourself accurately to other people and taking responsibility for your own actions.
    • Little Brother (a book by Cory Doctorow): Chapter 16 (A Confession to His Parents)
      Same scene as casel SA.4 above, from VIA's own honesty framing: presenting yourself accurately and taking responsibility, rather than continuing to manage a secret.
    • porttack.com: Society (Impact of Computing): Prove Me Wrong: A Socratic Seminar on AI and Cheating
      Honesty, in VIA's own sense of presenting yourself accurately and taking responsibility for your actions, is the lesson's explicit subject (a cheating scandal) and its own closing instruction to students: "I grade effort and honesty here, not the position landed on."
    • porttack.com: Socratic Seminar Kit: the source
      "I grade effort and honesty here, not the position landed on. 'I changed my mind because of what someone said' is the best possible answer" is the kit's own grading line, applying VIA's sense of honesty -- presenting yourself accurately, including when that means admitting you changed your mind -- to every seminar that uses this handout.
  • #COU.3 Perseverance Continuing to work toward a goal despite obstacles or setbacks.

    Unassigned

  • #COU.4 Zest Approaching life with energy and enthusiasm rather than going through the motions.

    Unassigned

#Humanity

Strengths that show up in caring for and befriending other people.

  • #HUM.1 Kindness Doing something genuinely helpful for someone else without expecting anything back.

    Unassigned

  • #HUM.2 Love Building close relationships defined by mutual care, not just shared proximity.
    • Little Brother (a book by Cory Doctorow): Chapter 17 (Barbara Stratford and the Crypto Wars) (partial)
      Ange's insistence on sharing Marcus's risk ('guilt by association... when you go, I go') is real mutual care under pressure, though the scene is a single commitment moment rather than the sustained relationship-building this code more fully describes.
  • #HUM.3 Social Intelligence Being aware of your own feelings and reading what the people around you are feeling too.

    Unassigned

#Justice

Strengths that underlie a healthy community life.

  • #JUS.1 Fairness Treating people equally and applying the same rules to everyone, not just the people you favor.

    Unassigned

  • #JUS.2 Leadership Guiding a group toward a shared goal while keeping relationships among its members healthy.

    Unassigned

  • #JUS.3 Teamwork Contributing your share to a group's effort and feeling responsible for the team's success, not just your own part.

    Unassigned

#Temperance

Strengths that protect against excess.

  • #TEM.1 Forgiveness Letting go of resentment toward someone who wronged you, without pretending it didn't happen.

    Unassigned

  • #TEM.2 Humility Knowing your own strengths without needing to be the center of attention for them.

    Unassigned

  • #TEM.3 Prudence Thinking ahead and avoiding unnecessary risk rather than acting on impulse.

    Unassigned

  • #TEM.4 Self-Regulation Managing your own impulses and actions on purpose, rather than being managed by them.

#Transcendence

Strengths that forge connections to the larger universe and provide meaning.

  • #TRA.1 Appreciation of Beauty and Excellence Noticing and genuinely responding to something well made or well done, whether it's art, nature, or someone else's skill.

    Unassigned

  • #TRA.2 Gratitude Noticing when someone has helped you and actually telling them so.

    Unassigned

  • #TRA.3 Hope Staying realistic about a situation while still expecting things to work out.

    Unassigned

  • #TRA.4 Humor Finding something to laugh about, including in a stressful moment, without making it at someone else's expense.

    Unassigned

  • #TRA.5 Spirituality Having a sense that your life fits into something larger than yourself.
