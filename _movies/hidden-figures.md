## What happens

In the early 1960s, Katherine Johnson, Dorothy Vaughan and Mary Jackson work at NASA's Langley center in Virginia as "computers," people who do the math by hand, in a segregated workplace. Katherine is moved to the team trying to get an astronaut into orbit. Mary fights to become an engineer, and Dorothy realizes a new IBM machine will replace the human computers and teaches herself and her team to program it. As NASA races the Soviets to launch John Glenn, all three have to prove they belong.

## Why we watch it

Hidden Figures is a true story about computing history: before there were electronic computers, a computer was a person, and these women were two steps ahead of the machine. It shows how they used math and programming to move NASA forward, and how they held on to their dignity when the rules were unfair. It is rated PG for thematic elements and some language. Common Sense Media lists it for ages 10 and up and rates the racism "a lot": segregation is shown realistically, including separate bathrooms, libraries and schools and a few period words. Some scenes are dramatized, and the teacher notes say which. The worksheet mixes computing and character.
