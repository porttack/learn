## What happens

People who used to work at Google, Facebook, Twitter, Pinterest and other tech companies explain how social media and search sites make money by holding people's attention, and how they use data and recommendation systems to do it. Their interviews are mixed with a fictional story about a family in which a teenage boy gets pulled deeper into his phone and a younger sister struggles with how she looks online. The film ends with a short list of things people can do.

## Why we watch it

This is a documentary about the technology that almost every student uses, told by people who helped build it. It gives students language for how recommendation systems, data and design choices work, and it is a good starting point for talking about habits and well-being. It is not neutral: critics praised the interviews but disliked the fictional segments, and Facebook has said the film gives a distorted view. That makes it good practice for asking who made something and what they want you to think. It is rated PG-13, and Common Sense Media lists it for ages 13 and up. It covers teen self-harm and suicide statistics. The worksheet has a computer science focus.
