## What happens

The film follows students and teachers through a school year at High Tech High, a public charter school in San Diego. There are no textbooks and no tests in the usual sense. Students work in teams on big projects, such as building a working model or putting on an exhibition for the public, and they are graded on what they make. We watch ninth graders struggle with a hard project, argue, miss deadlines and redo their work, and we see their teachers decide when to step in and when to let them figure it out. Between classroom scenes, the film asks whether the way most schools work still fits the world students are heading into.

## Why we watch it

Our robotics program runs on the same idea: you learn by building something real with a team, and things go wrong along the way. Students get to watch kids their own age deal with teamwork, setbacks and ownership of their work, and then think about how that compares to their own classes. It is a one-sided argument for project-based learning, so it is also good practice for asking what the filmmakers want you to believe and what they leave out.
