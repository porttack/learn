## What happens

Veritasium, a popular science channel on YouTube, visits ASML, a Dutch company that makes the machines that print the most advanced computer chips. The video explains Moore's Law (the number of transistors on a chip doubling about every two years), how chips are made in layers, why engineers needed a new kind of light called extreme ultraviolet to keep making them smaller, and how a machine shoots tiny drops of tin with a laser tens of thousands of times a second to make that light. It also tells the story of how a Dutch company, a German optics maker and several other companies and governments worked together to build it.

## Why we watch it

This is the hardware side of computer science: where the chips that run every program actually come from. It is a good way to show students that computing depends on physics, engineering and teamwork across countries, and that a few companies are very hard to replace. It is a 55-minute YouTube video made by Veritasium, not a feature film. It is not rated; it has no violence or swearing, and the topic is physics and engineering.
