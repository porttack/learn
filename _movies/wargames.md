## What happens

David Lightman is a bright but bored Seattle high school student with a home computer and a modem. While dialing phone numbers looking for new video games, he finds a computer that offers a list of games, including "Global Thermonuclear War." What he has actually reached is WOPR, a military supercomputer at NORAD that runs war simulations. When David plays, the computer treats the game as real, and the military starts to prepare for a real attack. David, his friend Jennifer, and the scientist who built the system's learning program have to stop it.

## Why we watch it

WarGames is a classic about hackers, passwords, and what happens when people hand big decisions to a computer. It is still very good at explaining ideas that matter now: a computer learns by trying things, a human should be able to stop it, and a harmless prank can turn into something serious fast. It is rated PG, but it was made in 1983 and has frequent swearing and a tense nuclear-war plot. Common Sense Media lists it for ages 11 and up. The worksheet has a computer science focus.
